This Week at Disney: A Union Vote, Piston Peak, EPCOT Menus, and New Merch

By Ryan · November 23, 2026

The short answer

Four things worth knowing this week: Walt Disney World's maintenance and trades unions voted down Disney's second contract offer, 735 to 231, and bargaining continues; the first vertical concrete is up for Cars Ridge Run Rally at Piston Peak; La Hacienda de San Angel and Le Cellier changed dishes and prices; and new merch includes a Walt Disney collection at Disneyland and a $69.99 Tree of Life sweatshirt at Animal Kingdom. None of this has changed published park hours.

A few things moved at once this week, and they're easy to mix together. Here's what's actually new, what it means if you're visiting, and what we still don't know.

The unions voted down Disney's second offer

Members of the Craft Maintenance Council rejected Walt Disney World's latest contract offer on Saturday, November 21. The count, in a release from the Southern Regional Council of Carpenters, was 231 yes and 735 no.

The council is a group of eight skilled-trade unions whose members include attraction mechanics, transportation maintenance, carpenters, painters, and other facilities roles — the people who keep the rides and the buildings running. Voting ran from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. on Friday and Saturday at the Embassy Suites on International Drive.

This was the second offer members have turned down. The current contract expired on September 28, 2026, and has been extended month to month while talks continue. A first offer was voted down on October 13. A strike-authorization vote that had been discussed for mid-November was set aside so members could vote on this revised proposal instead.

The carpenters' council says members are concerned about wages keeping up with inflation, Central Florida's cost of living, and health care costs. It also says bargaining will continue. The release does not set a new strike vote, and it does not announce a walkout.

What this means for guests right now: nothing official has changed about park hours or attraction operations because of this vote. These talks matter, because the work these teams do is what keeps the parks safe and open, but a rejected contract is not the same thing as a strike. I'll update this if either side announces a next step.

Piston Peak is finally going up, not just down

New aerial photos of Magic Kingdom, taken after a very wet week in Orlando, show the first vertical concrete for Cars Ridge Run Rally in Piston Peak National Park. Blog Mickey and WDW News Today, working from shots by bioreconstruct, both describe a tall concrete box rising near the Liberty Square retaining wall, with rebar still sticking out of the top, plus sloped decks stepping down toward the future creek.

The same photos show:

  • New retaining walls wrapping the old Liberty Square riverboat landing.
  • A deeper excavation on the Big Thunder Mountain side of the site.
  • Standing water in parts of the site after the rain, including in the channel where the land's stream is supposed to run past the Frontierland walkway.

Most of 2026 at this site has been foundations, pipes, and walls. This is the first clear move to building upward for the Cars ride. Disney still has not announced an opening date. Paperwork filed earlier this year pointed to infrastructure work for Piston Peak and neighboring Villains Land being complete by 2028, which is not the same thing as a guest opening.

If you're going to Magic Kingdom soon, you'll still be looking at construction walls, not a ride.

Two EPCOT restaurants changed their menus

Disney Food Blog documented a sizable menu swap at La Hacienda de San Angel in the Mexico pavilion, plus a smaller change at Le Cellier Steakhouse in Canada. I haven't eaten at either this week, so these are the published menu changes, not a taste test.

At Le Cellier, the Hand-cut Pomme Frites came off the sides list. In their place: Maple-Herb Sweet Potatoes with brown butter pistachio puree, a maple herb glaze, savory marshmallow, and crushed pistachios, for $10.50.

At La Hacienda de San Angel, several tacos and the Cowboy Ribeye for Two came off, and a few prices moved. The additions include:

  • Sopa de Lima, $13
  • Enmoladas de Pollo, $32
  • Parrillada Hacienda, $80, with shrimp +$12
  • Porterhouse, $125
  • Volcan de Chocolate, $16

Guacamole dropped from $16 to $13. A number of other starters, sides, and desserts went up a dollar or two, and Carne Asada moved from $54 to $56. If you have a reservation, skim the current menu before you go — the old taco list is not what you'll be ordering from.

New merch at both coasts

Two new collections showed up in shops this week.

At Disneyland Resort, WDW News Today found a Walt Disney-themed collection:

  • Disneyland Resort T-shirt, $39.99
  • Button-down shirt, $74.99
  • Zip-up sweatshirt, $69.99
  • Tote bag, $39.99
  • Dooney & Bourke crossbody, $248
  • Dooney & Bourke bucket bag, $268

The apparel and tote were spotted at Elias & Co. in Disney California Adventure. The Dooney & Bourke bags were at Disney Clothiers in Disneyland Park.

At Disney's Animal Kingdom, a Tree of Life crewneck sweatshirt is $69.99, with a wraparound tree print and the Animal Kingdom wordmark. It was found at Riverside Depot.

Stock in these shops moves quickly, and prices are as reported this week. If one of these is a must, go early or ask a Cast Member to check other locations.

Quick questions

Did the union vote authorize a strike?

No. Saturday's vote was on Disney's second contract offer. The unions said bargaining will continue. The vote did not announce a strike or a change in park operations.

Is there an opening date for Piston Peak?

Disney has not announced one. Construction is now going vertical for Cars Ridge Run Rally, but that's still a long way from a guest opening.
