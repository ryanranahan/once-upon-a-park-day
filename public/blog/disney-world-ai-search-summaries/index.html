Disney World's New AI Search Summaries: What They Are and How to Use Them

By Ryan · November 23, 2026

The short answer

Walt Disney World's website is rolling out a beta feature that can show an AI-generated Summary box above ordinary search results. Disney says the experience is powered by AI, can make mistakes, and saves summaries for quality assurance. Use it as a starting point, then open the official page or the My Disney Experience app before you act on hours, wait times, or menus. I have not been able to test the tool myself yet.

DisneyWorld.com is starting to show AI-generated summaries above the usual search results. If you use the site to look up dining, park hours, or character greetings, you may now see a box labeled Summary, with a Beta tag, before the regular list of pages.

I have not been able to run this tool myself, so this is an explainer, not a hands-on review. I'm not going to invent test queries or pretend I compared answers in the parks. What follows is what the feature is, how Disney is presenting it, and how I'd use it if it shows up on your screen.

What is it?

It's a short written answer that can appear at the top of search results on DisneyWorld.com. Disney has been talking about AI-powered search for a while, including in the My Disney Experience app, and the website version is now in a guest-facing beta.

The box is typically titled Summary. Longer answers may have a Show more control. Under the text, there's a Was this helpful? prompt with thumbs up and thumbs down. At the bottom, Disney includes a disclaimer along these lines: the experience is powered by AI and can make mistakes, and summaries are saved for quality assurance.

That last part matters. If you type a question, don't assume it's private. Disney says it keeps the summaries to review quality.

This is not the same thing as Disney's separate vacation-planning experience, which helps some guests compare resorts and build trip ideas. The Summary box lives in ordinary website search — the one you already use to find a restaurant page or an hours calendar.

How would I use it?

If the box appears, I'd treat it like a smart table of contents, not like a final answer.

  • Ask a specific question. "Where can I get a quick-service lunch in EPCOT?" is more useful than "food."
  • Read the summary, then open the official page it points to. Many answers will send you to a dining page, the park hours calendar, or a transportation page.
  • Check anything time-sensitive in the My Disney Experience app. Hours, wait times, menus, and Mobile Order availability change. A website summary can lag.
  • Use the thumbs up or down. That's how Disney is collecting feedback during the beta.
  • Keep scrolling. The regular results are still underneath. Sometimes those are what you actually need.

I would not use a summary alone to lock in a dining reservation, skip a standby confirmation, or assume a refurbishment date is current.

Where can it go wrong?

Disney is already telling you it can make mistakes. A few common ways a travel-planning summary goes wrong, even when it sounds confident:

  • Hours and showtimes change. A summary that was right this morning can be wrong tonight.
  • Similar names get mixed up. Parks, restaurants, and seasonal events often share words.
  • The answer may be incomplete. A "partial list" of restaurants or attractions is not the whole list.
  • It may not appear at all. During a beta, some guests see it and some don't, and some queries may never trigger a box.

If a summary and the official page disagree, trust the official page or the app.

Should you change how you plan a trip?

Not really. This is a convenience layer on top of the same site. It might save you a click when you're trying to remember which park has a certain snack, or how to get from Hollywood Studios to EPCOT. It should not replace checking reservations, walking times, or the calendar for your actual travel dates.

If you're still early in planning, start with our planning guides and then use Disney's own site and app to confirm the details for your days. A summary can help you find the right page faster. It shouldn't be the page you plan from.

Quick questions

Is this the same thing as Disney's vacation planning tool?

No. The Summary box sits inside regular DisneyWorld.com search. Disney also has a separate vacation-planning experience, sometimes called Guided Search, that helps with trip ideas and resort comparisons.

Will I see it every time I search?

Not necessarily. It's still in beta, and it may not appear for every guest or every query while testing continues.

Can I trust the answers?

Treat them as a starting point. Disney itself warns that the feature can make mistakes. Confirm hours, wait times, menus, and reservations on the official page or in the My Disney Experience app.
