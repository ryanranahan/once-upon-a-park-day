Disney's Grand Floridian Resort & Spa

A Victorian-style seaside hotel on the Seven Seas Lagoon, and Walt Disney World's flagship resort.

By Ryan · Stayed here 20+ times since 2018 · Facts last checked November 2026

Our take

I've stayed at the Grand Floridian more than 20 times over the last eight years, mostly on my own for work and also with my wife. It still impresses me every time I walk in. The lobby is huge and elegant in a way that tells you right away you're at Disney's flagship resort.

Is it worth it? For me, yes: you're paying for a great location next to Magic Kingdom and big, comfortable rooms. Every restaurant here is good, and just walking around the grounds is a treat because everything is kept pristine.

Who it suits

Couples and families who want a classic, elegant Disney stay right next to Magic Kingdom.

Rooms

I've had just about every type of view here. My favorites are the lagoon view and the theme park view. With a theme park view you can watch the fireworks from your balcony, and the resort pipes in the show music, so it feels like you're standing in Magic Kingdom.

The rooms are nice, and I've seen Disney refresh them a few times over the years, which they needed.

The one room to avoid: first-floor rooms outside the pool storage closet. There's no view, and you won't want to use your balcony.

Check your building before you book. Not every room is in the main building. Several outer buildings surround it, so look at the view category and building, not just the price.

Pools and things to do

The pools are large, with plenty of room for you and your family. They're genuinely nice.

There are two main pools. The Courtyard Pool is the calmer one, with a zero-entry edge. The Beach Pool is livelier, with a waterslide and a splash area for kids.

Where to eat

Two favorites: Narcoossee's, where the steak is very good, and 1900 Park Fare, which has a little bit of everything at breakfast and dinner. Park Fare is well worth it, and you get time with characters too.

For quick service, you can't go wrong with Gasparilla Island Grill.

My mobile order trick: place your order before you get there, then tap "I'm here" when you're about four minutes away. Your food is ready right as you walk in.

A few more things worth knowing:

  • Book table-service and character meals as soon as your booking window opens. Disney resort guests get an earlier window than day visitors.
  • Arrive 10 to 15 minutes early for reservations. The resort is spread out, and the whole party has to be there before you're seated.
  • Dress codes: Victoria & Albert's is formal and for ages 10 and up, and Narcoossee's asks for no swimsuits, tank tops or cut-offs.
  • At 1900 Park Fare, look up. Big Bertha, a century-old band organ, plays above the dining room.
  • Afternoon tea at the Garden View Lounge needs a reservation and has a strict 24-hour cancellation policy.
  • The Perch, a newer lobby bar, is a nice spot for a drink while the lobby musicians play.

Getting to the parks

  • Monorail to Magic Kingdom and Epcot (Epcot via a transfer at the Transportation and Ticket Center)
  • Boat to Magic Kingdom
  • Walking path to Magic Kingdom
  • Buses to Hollywood Studios, Animal Kingdom, and Disney Springs

I usually recommend the monorail, but the walkway is great too. The boat is just a fun way to get to Magic Kingdom, with the best views.

  • The boat can pause for bad weather, so don't build a tight schedule around it.
  • Hollywood Studios and Animal Kingdom are bus-only from here. The monorail doesn't go there.
  • As a Deluxe resort guest you get Early Theme Park Entry, plus Extended Evening Hours on select nights. Check what applies to your dates.

Pros and cons

Love

  • The rooms, especially with a lagoon or theme park view
  • The property itself: elegant, beautifully kept, and great for a stroll
  • Walking to and from Magic Kingdom, and over to the Polynesian

Keep in mind

  • It's one of the priciest resorts on property.
  • Some rooms are a long walk from the lobby.
  • Renovation work has been reported to continue into early 2027, so check what's affected before your trip.

First-timer tips

  • Watch the fireworks from the resort at least once.
  • Don't miss the walkway. Lots of people don't know you can walk to Magic Kingdom and the Polynesian.
  • You can still take the monorail to Epcot from here. Just transfer at the Transportation and Ticket Center.
  • Find a fireworks spot outside your room: the beach near Gasparilla Island Grill or the boat dock. The Electrical Water Pageant also floats past in the evening.
